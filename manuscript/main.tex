\begin{abstract}
Local-currency depreciation can reduce the USD repayment burden of debt without establishing the safety of a lending protocol. We develop a reproducible counterfactual credit-loss stress test for Argentine-peso (ARS) and Turkish-lira (TRY) lending backed by ETH or BTC. Empirical market inputs comprise 42 joint monthly returns, constructed from official monthly-average exchange rates and monthly averages of daily crypto reference prices over January 2020--July 2023. The initial collateral distribution, auction frictions, rate policies and funding architecture are illustrative assumptions, not observed local-currency lending conditions. Under a three-month moving-block bootstrap, the baseline adaptive rule and timely auctions produce expected liquidated-exposure shares of \ArsLiq{} for ARS/ETH and \TryLiq{} for TRY/ETH. Mean credit losses are \ArsLoss{} and \TryLoss{} of initial principal; 99\% conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) is \ArsTail{} and \TryTail{}. Close-out extensions prevent delayed liquidations from disappearing at the horizon. Principal-weight ablations show that MakerDAO draw sizes serve a scale illustration rather than percentage-loss calibration. Within the tested policy grid, rate pass-through trades off financing-side debt relief against liquidation and credit loss. Results depend materially on monthly measurement, the short calibration sample and auction assumptions. Credit-budget exhaustion, a stylized matched-funding accounting deficit and stablecoin redemption failure are distinct outcomes; this study does not forecast peg stability or establish protocol viability.
\end{abstract}
\textbf{Keywords:} DeFi; local-currency debt; credit loss; liquidation; foreign exchange; counterfactual stress testing.

\section{Introduction}
Collateralised decentralized-finance lending combines a loan claim, pledged crypto collateral and a token or funding obligation. Smart-contract accounting makes these links explicit, but does not eliminate credit, liquidity or governance risks \cite{Schar2021,Werner2022,MakerVatDocs}. A local-currency unit adds an important distinction: depreciation against USD reduces the translated value of both an LCU loan and a matching LCU liability. It is therefore incorrect to infer a protocol loss directly from a borrower's USD debt erosion.

We study a hypothetical architecture in which the protocol advances one LCU loan funded by an equal, non-interest-bearing LCU token liability. One token promises one LCU, not one USD. The loan accrues interest, while the original funding principal does not. Crypto collateral remains pledged borrower property until sale; it must not also be counted as an unencumbered protocol asset. USD reserves supplied by equity investors absorb a settlement deficit. If those reserves are inadequate, the model reports an unfunded accounting gap; it does not assume successful governance-token issuance or peg restoration. Redemption is assumed to coincide with loan settlement at the model FX rate. On-demand redemption, runs and executable exchange-market access are outside this stylized funding diagnostic.

\textbf{Primary estimand and unit.} The primary estimand is expected cumulative contractual credit loss after collateral recovery, divided by initial USD principal, for a synthetic currency--collateral portfolio over a 12-month origination horizon plus any scheduled liquidation close-out. A unit of simulation is one joint market path applied to an assumed distribution of initial collateral ratios (CRs), not an observed MakerDAO borrower.

The questions are how lagged rate policies alter debt growth and liquidation, how auction frictions and collateral buffers change losses, and how much these outcomes vary with market measurement and calibration. The contribution is an integrated, reproducible accounting experiment. It combines monthly first-breach rules with explicit delayed settlement, a proceeds waterfall, separate survivor and liquidated-borrower measures, and matched-liability diagnostics. It makes no universal impossibility claim and provides no regulatory capital recommendation.

\section{Related literature and incremental contribution}
Empirical DeFi lending studies establish that rates, utilization and borrower activity are state-dependent \cite{Gudgeon2020Funds,Saengchote2023,Cornelli2025}. Liquidation studies show that collateral thresholds and execution incentives matter, and that observed liquidations interact with market liquidity and price volatility \cite{Qin2021Liquidations,Perez2021,SasiBrodesky2023}. Evidence on MakerDAO resilience and oracle design motivates attention to collateral valuation and execution \cite{Kjaer2021,Eskandari2021}. These results justify modelling frictions; they do not identify the numerical haircut or congestion coefficient for a hypothetical ARS or TRY protocol.

Stablecoin research distinguishes backing from secondary-market stability: arbitrage, endogenous collateral dynamics and redemption institutions all matter \cite{Lyons2023,KlagesMundt2022,GortonZhang2023}. A credit-loss calculation cannot replace a run model. Emerging-market research highlights currency substitution and financial-stability exposures \cite{BIS2023,Copestake2023}. Evidence connecting stablecoin flows and FX markets further motivates treating official FX as a reference unit rather than a guaranteed conversion price \cite{Aldasoro2026}.

Table~\ref{tab:gap} selects representative works for their relevance to a particular model channel, not as an exhaustive review of each field. The novelty is the integration and auditability of those channels under local-currency denomination, not the invention of liquidation stress testing, bootstrap inference or stablecoin balance-sheet accounting.

Five concepts must be separated. (i) Contractual credit loss is debt minus loan recovery. (ii) FX translation changes the USD loan value. (iii) Matching token liabilities translate with the same FX factor, offsetting the principal component. (iv) Peg or redemption shortfall also depends on timing, liquidity and market conversion; it is not estimated here. (v) Overall protocol insolvency would require all assets and obligations, valuation rules and contingent liabilities. We therefore use \emph{credit loss} throughout and reserve the term \emph{accounting gap} for the deliberately restricted funding ledger.

\subsection{Debt and liquidation equations}
For monthly returns $\delta_t$ and $g_t$, and a nominal annual rate $r_t$, the explicit recursions are
\begin{align}
X_t&=X_{t-1}(1+\delta_t),&Z_t&=Z_{t-1}(1+g_t),\\
U_t&=U_{t-1}(1+r_t/12),&U_0&=X_0=Z_0=1,\\
D_{sjt}&=U_{s,t}/X_{s,t},&C_{sjt}&=c_jZ_{s,t},\qquad CR_{sjt}=C_{sjt}/D_{sjt}.
\end{align}
The rate convention is nominal annual divided by 12, not effective annual compounding. First breach uses the strict inequality $CR<\theta$. A breach in months 1--12 is irrevocable and schedules full collateral sale in month $\tau_j+d$, even if the CR later recovers. Interest accrues until execution. Unbreached positions repay in month 12. No new loans or breaches are originated after month 12; months 13--14 only close scheduled sales.

For exposure sold in month $t$, $q_{s,t}=\sum_jw_j\ind(e_{sj}=t,\tau_j\leq12)$, and
\begin{align}
h_{s,t}&=\min(\bar h,h_0+\gamma q_{s,t}),\quad \bar h=0.30,\\
a_{sj}&=(1-h_{s,e_{sj}})C_{sj,e_{sj}},\\
R_{sj}&=\min(D_{sj,e_{sj}},a_{sj}),\quad L_{sj}=D_{sj,e_{sj}}-R_{sj},\\
\pi_{sj}&=\min(0.13D_{sj,e_{sj}},[a_{sj}-D_{sj,e_{sj}}]_+),\\
V_{sj}&=[a_{sj}-D_{sj,e_{sj}}-\pi_{sj}]_+.
\end{align}
Here $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$. Penalty is paid to the protocol only from proceeds remaining after loan recovery; it cannot increase loan recovery above debt or create a fictitious cash receipt. The auction haircut represents value lost to execution frictions or auction participants. For survivors, $R=D$, $L=\pi=h=0$, and collateral remains with the borrower. The liquidation waterfall satisfies $a=R+\pi+V$ exactly.

The financing-side net debt effect is
\begin{equation}
b_{sj}=1-\left(R_{sj}+\pi_{sj}+h_{s,e_{sj}}C_{sj,e_{sj}}\right).
\end{equation}
For liquidation this equals $1-C+V$; for survival it is $1-D_{sj,12}$. It measures normalized initial financing proceeds less resources surrendered in settlement, including auction friction. It excludes pre-sale collateral investment returns, taxes, conversion costs, peg deviations, discounting, post-settlement reinvestment and borrowing utility. Default can reduce repayment, so it is not a welfare, profit or general borrower-benefit measure. Liquidated and surviving positions have different holding periods; we report both separately.

\subsection{Funding and reserve diagnostics}
Gross portfolio credit loss is $\ell_s=\sum_j w_jL_{sj}$. Its credit-budget indicator is $\ind(\ell_s>k)$, not an insolvency event. In the separate matched-funding diagnostic, outstanding principal liabilities are retired at each position's settlement FX rate. The cash-capital ledger is
\begin{equation}
K_{s,t}=k+\sum_{j:e_{sj}\leq t}w_j\left(R_{sj}+\pi_{sj}-X_{s,e_{sj}}^{-1}\right).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, each settled contribution is $(U-1)/X-L+\pi$. Thus the loan and liability principal translation cancels; interest is not assumed to offset loss until actually recovered. A negative ledger flags a funding gap, even if it later recovers. Negative balances are recorded as unfunded gaps, not assumed external borrowing. Before all loans settle, this cash ledger is not total marked-to-market equity; unrealized loan surplus is excluded. After all settlement it equals residual equity for this restricted architecture. USD conversion at official reference FX and synchronized liability maturity are strong assumptions. Early token redemption, liquidity runs, operational costs and endogenous token prices are not simulated.

\section{Data, construction and parameter provenance}
\subsection{Market observations and alignment}
Archived OECD/FRED ARS and TRY monthly-average series provide LCU per USD \cite{FREDARS,FREDTRY}. Coin Metrics daily \texttt{PriceUSD} observations are averaged within each calendar month \cite{CoinMetricsData}. There are 43 level observations, January 2020--July 2023, yielding 42 returns. This revision replaces the original mixture of monthly-average FX and month-end crypto. Both main series now use monthly averages, although business-day FX and calendar-day crypto sampling are not identical. These are low-frequency reference marks, not simultaneous executable prices; monthly averaging also suppresses intramonth extremes.

Official rates may differ substantially from accessible conversion prices, especially under Argentine exchange controls. No parallel-rate observations are invented or substituted. This is an official-rate counterfactual, and its conclusions should not be interpreted as returns achievable by an Argentine retail borrower. The archived analysis period is held fixed for traceability. Subperiods and leave-one-year-out analyses test sample dependence; a longer modern sample remains a distinct future data extension.

This revision uses its archived 100 principal-bin summaries as an inherited scale and concentration illustration. Raw blockchain decoding was not repeated for this revision. The input-preparation script can rebuild those summaries from the documented companion event file and verifies its checksum when supplied.

Let $v_b$ be the principal share of bin $b$ and $w_j$ the assumed CR exposure weight. The synthetic product portfolio has weights $v_bw_j$ across $100\times31=3,100$ cells. Principal size is assumed independent of CR and market path; all dollar amounts scale linearly and congestion depends on a share. Therefore for any normalized cohort outcome $f_j$,
\begin{equation}
\sum_b\sum_jv_bw_j f_j=\sum_j w_j f_j,\qquad \sum_b v_b=1.
\end{equation}
This is exact scale homogeneity under the stated model, not empirical evidence that size never matters. Dollar-dependent slippage or correlated size and leverage would break it.

\section{Stress-test design and event order}
The main bootstrap samples joint FX--crypto pairs in non-circular blocks of three consecutive observations. Starting indices are uniform over $0,\ldots,n-B$; blocks that extend beyond the historical sample are never eligible. Blocks are concatenated and the final partial block is truncated to 14 months. The same 14-month paths are used for all rate and auction comparisons within a currency--collateral pair. No path extension is deterministically extrapolated. Alternative block lengths 1 and 6 use their own fixed seeds; the block-3 robustness row reuses the main run exactly.

Each month: (1) observe reference FX and crypto prices; (2) determine rates using only prior FX returns; (3) accrue unpaid debt; (4) calculate CRs; (5) register first strict threshold breaches through month 12; (6) queue them for the specified execution delay; (7) execute due full-collateral sales and determine congestion; (8) allocate proceeds to debt, penalty and borrower residual, and record loss; (9) retire matching funding, update the capital ledger and borrower outcomes. Unbreached loans repay at month 12; scheduled sales continue through month 14. A standalone one-page algorithm accompanies the revision.

The historical diagnostic uses every complete 14-month market window: 29 overlapping windows per currency--collateral pair. Each provides 12 months for origination and two for possible close-out. A common available-window set avoids comparing timely full windows with shorter delayed samples. These windows overlap and are not independent observations.

Twenty thousand simulated paths reduce numerical integration error conditional on 42 monthly observations. They do not create additional economic observations. We add calendar-aware leave-one-year-out analysis (blocks cannot cross a deleted calendar year), early and late subperiods, an IID bivariate Gaussian log-return benchmark with sample-estimated mean and covariance, and outer block resampling of the 42-return calibration. The Gaussian benchmark preserves contemporaneous covariance but not serial dependence or fat tails; it is a model comparison, not a preferred forecast. Outer-resampling percentile ranges combine calibration perturbations with residual inner simulation noise. With only 100 outer replicates they are descriptive sensitivity bands, not coverage-validated confidence intervals. Joint parameter ranges represent scenario uncertainty rather than probabilities over parameters.

\section{Rate rules and diagnostics}
For $m_t=\min(k,t-1)$ prior returns and $t>1$, define
\begin{equation}
A_{t,k}=\max\left\{\exp\left[\frac{12}{m_t}\sum_{u=t-m_t}^{t-1}\log(1+\delta_u)\right]-1,0\right\}.
\end{equation}
All rules start at $r_1=0.20$ and use expanding history until their full lookback is available; no preceding unobserved return is fabricated. Policies are static 20\%; adaptive $\operatorname{clip}(0.10+0.75A_{t,3},0.05,1.50)$; indexed $\operatorname{clip}(0.05+A_{t,3},0.05,2.00)$; lower-cap adaptive with a 0.60 ceiling; and trailing-12 adaptive with $k=12$ and a 0.60 ceiling. Partial adjustment uses the lower-cap target $r_t^*$ and
\begin{equation}
r_t=r_{t-1}+\operatorname{clip}\{0.25(r_t^*-r_{t-1}),-0.05,0.05\}.
\end{equation}
Thus governance moves at most five percentage points per month. The trailing-12 policy has fewer than 12 past observations within the initial origination year; full history becomes available only in close-out. We label that initialization explicitly rather than implying a pre-existing 12-month history.

\section{Results and denominators}
\subsection{Main outcomes}
Liquidated exposure is $\E[\sum_jw_j\ind(\tau_j\leq12)]$, including all scheduled sales after close-out. Mean credit loss is $\E[\ell_s]$ relative to initial principal. CVaR$_{99}$ is the average of the largest 1\% of path-level losses (200 observations in a 20,000-path run). For non-multiples of 100, the implementation uses the largest $\lceil.01N\rceil$ losses. The probability of \emph{any} portfolio liquidation is a different statistic and can be large because a very small positive cohort weight is close to the threshold. It is not an individual borrower's liquidation probability. Cohort-specific probabilities are provided in \texttt{cohort\_results.csv}; averaging them with $w_j$ gives the probability for a representative normalized-principal position drawn from the assumed CR distribution, not an empirically identified MakerDAO position.

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports six rate rules under timely auctions. The baseline adaptive mean credit loss is \ArsLoss{} for ARS/ETH and \TryLoss{} for TRY/ETH, with tail loss \ArsTail{} and \TryTail{}. Higher pass-through increases liquidation in these cases, while partial adjustment attenuates that response. These are comparisons within the tested design space. A three-way tension among financing relief, rate indexation and low credit loss is not proof that every possible protocol must face the same frontier.

\section{Robustness and uncertainty}
\subsection{Market measurement and short history}
Using the old mixed monthly convention with the revised engine produces mean losses of \ArsMixedLoss{} and \TryMixedLoss{}, versus \ArsLoss{} and \TryLoss{} with monthly averages for both series. Measurement choice is therefore material; the old numbers cannot be retained as invariant conclusions. Appendix tables report block lengths, omitted years, subperiods and the parametric benchmark. Omitting 2022 sharply reduces losses in both currencies; this short history is economically consequential. The parametric model can yield different tail estimates despite fitting first and second log-return moments.

\section{Design implications and limitations}
The demonstrated implications are narrow: reporting a gross credit budget as ``protocol solvency'' obscures funding assumptions; testing auction haircut independently of delay improves interpretation; rate caps and partial adjustment change debt growth; and a short historical calibration produces substantial input uncertainty. An initial-principal ceiling derived solely from CVaR is not labelled ``safe'' here. Diversified auction participants, circuit breakers and backstop liquidity could change the assumed haircut or execution mechanism, but their efficacy is not tested and requires separate investigation.

Several constraints remain. Monthly-average reference marks miss intramonth triggers and executable price paths. Official FX may be inaccessible. The 42-month calibration contains unusual economic regimes, and block resampling assumes enough local stationarity to reuse them. The parameter grid is a design exercise, not an estimated population distribution. Principal and leverage are independent by assumption, while real borrowers can adjust collateral, repay, refinance or default strategically. Auctions sell the entire position, do not simulate order books or induced price feedback, and impose an illustrative linear congestion rule. The funding diagnostic excludes early redemption, endogenous stablecoin prices, legal priority, operational costs, cross-currency hedges and governance recapitalization. Longer data, an executable FX dataset and a separately specified redemption/run model would be required for stronger claims.

Data alignment, initialization, close-out censoring, cohort alternatives and parameter sensitivity have been addressed directly rather than left as disclaimers. This does not remove their residual modelling uncertainty. The revised numerical estimates supersede the submitted version's headline values.

\section{Conclusion}
This study provides a transparent counterfactual credit-loss framework linking local-currency debt growth, crypto collateral and liquidation settlement. On the stated monthly-average calibration, the adaptive timely-auction mean losses are \ArsLoss{} and \TryLoss{} for ARS/ETH and TRY/ETH. Principal-weight invariance clarifies the descriptive role of MakerDAO draw sizes, while close-out accounting distinguishes pending sales from executed liquidations. Measurement, economic subperiod and auction assumptions materially affect results. The contribution is a reproducible stress-testing method and an explicit accounting boundary, not evidence that an actual local-currency lending protocol is viable, solvent or safe.
